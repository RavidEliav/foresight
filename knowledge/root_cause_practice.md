# Verifying a hypothesis in manufacturing

## From association to cause
Statistical findings from production data are associations. Turning one into a confirmed cause
needs evidence that links the process condition to the failure mechanism, ideally evidence that
the failure rate changes when the condition is changed.

## Typical verification steps
Engineers usually start with records that already exist: equipment and alarm logs, maintenance
and part-replacement history, recipe change records and material lot genealogy. If failures
line up with a tool, a chamber, a maintenance window or a material lot, the hypothesis becomes
stronger.

## Controlled experiments
The strongest test is a designed experiment, for example splitting a lot between two settings
or two chambers and comparing the results. Designed experiments separate the effect of the
tested factor from everything else that changes over time.

## Structured problem solving
Structured methods such as cause-and-effect (fishbone) diagrams and the 8D process help list
candidate causes, rank them by evidence and record what was checked, so that the team does not
stop at the first plausible explanation.
