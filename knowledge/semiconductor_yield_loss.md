# Semiconductor yield loss

## Two broad kinds of yield loss
Yield loss is often split into defect-related loss, where particles or local defects kill
individual dies, and parametric or systematic loss, where a process step is slightly off target
and whole wafers or lots drift toward the edge of their electrical specifications. Random
defects tend to scatter across wafers; systematic problems tend to follow a tool, a chamber, a
recipe, a time window or a wafer region.

## Usual sources of systematic problems
Typical sources include equipment drift and wear between maintenance events, chamber-to-chamber
differences on the same tool, recipe or setting changes, consumables reaching the end of their
life, changes in incoming materials or chemicals, and environmental changes in the cleanroom.

## Why sensor data helps
Process tools record many sensor readings per run, such as temperatures, pressures, gas flows,
power and timing. When a failure is linked to a process condition, some of these readings tend
to differ between failing and passing runs. A difference shows where to look; confirming a cause
needs engineering follow-up such as equipment logs, maintenance records or a controlled
experiment.

## Many small effects
In complex manufacturing, failures are often caused by combinations of small deviations rather
than one large fault. This is one reason why single-sensor statistics and simple models can
show weak predictive power even when the data is informative.
